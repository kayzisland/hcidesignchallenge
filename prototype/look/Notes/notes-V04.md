## The headline points

1. **Big, saturated feedback after a wrong answer caused sensory overload and frustration.** Blocks' huge "Not quite" would "piss him off" by the third wrong answer *(recalled; "infuriating" in the live note is Jishnu's paraphrase)*. It makes being wrong glaringly obvious instead of explaining why. He wanted Blocks' layout with Soft Focus's calm treatment: light colours, and the same colours for right and wrong.
2. **The post-lecture nudge needs a student-set delay.** Straight after a lecture he would swipe it away and find it "really annoying". He suggested letting the student choose how long after the lecture it arrives, up to 90 minutes, to allow for commuting. He studies after the commute; others might prefer the bus *(recalled)*.
3. **Week: the planner layout matches a mental model he already has.** "it's what I'm used to and it's easy to use" *(recalled, word for word)*: his secondary school planner. The study target matters: he wants to "know what I'm working towards", and called it "the main thing the app is supposed to address", because "a reminder is good, everyone needs a reminder" *(recalled)*.
4. **Colour: bright but soft.** Pastel rather than deep, saturated colour (a pastel orange, not Blocks' deep orange). Dark backgrounds with bright white text were too harsh; plain white backgrounds lacked contrast *(recalled)*.
5. **Accessibility problem in Blocks:** he could not read the orange "I don't know" option on the Practise question; it blended into the background *(recalled)*. He wondered if that was deliberate, since "Show me first" was much clearer.
6. **Progress should be plain and quantitative.** Preferred Paper; Blocks was overwhelming. He didn't understand what the pie chart represented, and the circles didn't show his progress legibly either. He preferred bars and numbers *(recalled)*.
7. **Text-heavy screens read like "a research paper"** (his words). If he wanted to read a paper he'd read one; it felt confusing *(recalled)*.

## Looking back

Layout: Blocks · Amount on screen: Blocks · Menu: Soft Focus · Numbers: Paper · Buttons and boxes: Blocks · Text: Soft Focus · Colours: Soft Focus · Icons: Blocks · **Whole app: Blocks**
One word each: Paper "Formal", Blocks "Vibrant", Timeline "Practical", Soft Focus "Purple".

- *Live notes:* who is it for: "yes it'd be helpful". Hard to read: Timeline in places. One change to Blocks: less bright colours; better icon positions, as in Soft Focus. Blocks best layout but colours too strong; Soft Focus clean and practical but doesn't stand out; Soft Focus's side menu best, Blocks' top menu not practical; Progress: some good parts of Blocks, but Paper overall.
- *(recalled)* Soft Focus: "This design reminds me of Seneca, in fact it's exactly like Seneca, it's literally the same". A layout he has seen many times. "Meh" wasn't meant badly: by the end he quite liked it, but it didn't stand out the way Blocks did, so he'd like a mix of the two.
- *(recalled)* Menu: preferred Soft Focus's vertical side menu; a menu along the top, as in Blocks, felt less intuitive.
- *(recalled)* Timeline's hard-to-read parts were its small labels and grey text. Jishnu notes other testers raised the same; check their own records before counting it across testers.
- *(recalled)* Blocks would suit him if combined with parts of Soft Focus and one part of Paper, matching his end picks: Blocks for layout, amount on screen, buttons and icons; Soft Focus for menu, text and colours; Paper for numbers.
- *(recalled)* Called Blocks "unserious", though it was his overall pick. What made it so: everything was very "poppy", a word he used several times.
- *(recalled)* Would use it: reminders, and syncing calendars and timetables; he could "see people like himself using it for sure" (paraphrase).
