# Look test run sheet: Fri 27 Nov (lo-fi cycle 2)

Nov 27, 2026 · @Jishnu (facilitator). Builds on Jiven's look test (`README.md`) and QOC (`../../ideate/visual-design-space.md`).

## What today is for

The QOC found that almost nothing about how LockedIn looks has been tested. Today, students compare four looks for the same seven screens. Content and features are identical in all four, so only the look differs. Each tester's answers feed one rule, written before the first tester, which decides for each part of the look (layout, menu, numbers, colour and so on) whether to adopt an option, make it a setting, or keep what's built. Jiven builds the result into the hi-fi at T24.

It does **not** test whether LockedIn helps anyone study, or whether the features work. The look test only shows stills, so motion (QOC Q9) isn't tested either.

## One session (25 to 30 minutes)

| Step | What you do | Time |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Consent | Read the consent script on the start page, word for word. Tick only after a clear yes. No audio or screen recording unless you ask separately and they say yes | 2 min |
| 2. Details | Tester ID, "What do you study?", year, STEM or not, in person or online. Never their name | 1 min |
| 3. Scenario | Read the "Then say" text aloud: it sets Thursday just after 1 pm and asks them to think aloud. Press **Start the test** | 1 min |
| 4. Seven screens | For each screen, two steps (below) | 17 min |
| 5. Looking back | Eight picks, one per part of the look; the whole app; one word per design | 3 min |
| 6. Closing questions | Four questions on the last page | 2 min |
| 7. Save | **Finish → Copy results**, paste into `interview_results.md`, then **Next tester** | 1 min |

**Each screen, step 1: one design alone.**
1. "What do you notice first?" Type what they say in their words. Don't point at anything.
2. Press **Ask the reading question** as you start reading it out. That starts the timer.
3. Keep the answer folded. Peek only to mark it. Mark **Right**, **Partly, or with help**, or **Wrong, or gave up**.
4. Press **Show all four**.

**Each screen, step 2: all four together.** Give them a moment to look. Ask the three questions as written: which would you rather use, which is most cluttered, which puts the most pressure on you. "None" is a fine answer. Then ask why for each pick and type their reasons.

## Running a session online

All of today's sessions are online. The page runs on the facilitator's laptop only. **Never send testers the HTML file:** its Browse page names the design that's already built, the answers are one click away, and the facilitator is the one who marks the reading question.

**The tester window.** On the start page, press **Open tester window**. A second window opens that shows the tester only the designs: one design on its own, then the four side by side, then the end strip. It never shows the prompts, the answer, the marks or your notes. **In Teams, share that window only.** Run the whole test from the main window, which stays on your side. "Open full size" in the main window also shows that design full size in the tester window. If nothing opens, allow pop-ups for the page and press the button again.

**Before the call**
- Ask the tester to join from a laptop or desktop, not a phone, because the screens are too small to judge on a phone.
- Turn on Do Not Disturb. Open the tester window and make it large; put the main window where you can see it but don't share it.
- Keep the answer key below to hand. With the tester window shared, unfolding the answer on the main page is safe.
- Don't record, and turn off Teams transcription, unless you ask separately and they say yes to that too.

**During the call**
- Read the consent script aloud from the main window, then tick consent. The tester window says "The test will start in a moment" until you press **Start the test**.
- **"Show me" means "tell me".** Two reading questions ask where they'd tap. Online, say "Tell me where you'd click", and mark it Right if they describe it clearly enough that you could click it yourself.
- The four side by side are smaller over a call. Open each with **Open full size**, one by one, before asking the three questions. Allow 30 to 35 minutes.
- Ask "What are you thinking?" more often than in person, because you can't see where they're looking.
- The tester window says "That's everything. Thank you!" at the end, so you can copy the results without stopping the share.

## Facilitator rules

- **Read every script word for word.** Same consent, scenario and questions for everyone.
- **Never say which design is the one already built,** or which one you like. If asked, say "I'll tell you at the end."
- **Don't help with the reading question.** If they ask, say "What would you expect?" Step in only after about a minute stuck, and mark it "Partly, or with help".
- **Don't defend a design.** If they criticise one, say "That's useful" and move on.
- **Keep them talking:** "What are you thinking?"
- **Record only what happened.** A blank stays blank. No estimating, merging testers or rewording quotes.

## After the last session: tonight's write-up

1. **Tally.** Open **Tally all testers**, paste the whole of `interview_results.md` into the box, and check the tester list at the top is right (no PILOT, no duplicates).
2. **Read the rule's suggestions, then decide as a group.** For each part of the look the page says one of three things:
   - **Adopt:** at least 4 of 5 (70% of a larger group) chose the same design, and no reading result points the other way.
   - **No winner:** make it a setting if their reasons were about taste, otherwise keep what's built (Soft Focus).
   - **Too few answers:** fewer than 5 testers answered. Say so; don't adopt anything yet.

   Screen by screen, an option is **ruled out** where a majority called it the most cluttered or the most pressuring there, or where most testers who met it first got the reading question wrong. The tally marks reading flags with fewer than 3 testers as too few to act on.
3. **Copy the tally as Markdown** into a results file, for example `test/look-results.md`, with the limitations below.
4. **Give each finding an ID** (`LF2-1`, `LF2-2` … for the second lo-fi cycle, if the group agrees) and log each change in `design-log.md` with the finding behind it.
5. **Send Jiven the per-part outcomes** in priority order for T24.

## Limitations to declare

- **Stills, not use.** Nobody studies in a mock-up, and motion isn't shown.
- **Small numbers.** Report counts ("4 of 5"), never percentages. Each design is met first only one or two times per screen across all testers, so reading results are early signals.
- **A preference for a design doesn't say which part earned it.** The eight per-part picks are how we separate them, roughly.
- **The look was explored after the hi-fi was styled.** The QOC is the repair, and the deck should say so.
- **The question set changed after two testers.** V01 and V02 used v1 questions; two of their seven reading results each are left out of the tally.
- **Convenience sample:** students we could reach, possibly kinder than strangers.
