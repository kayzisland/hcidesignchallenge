  8. Done: apps back, what's next, no penalty for gaps

Why this flow

  • The 10 Nov sync made starting the core problem (Q-1, AFF-A).
  • The engine (questions, confidence) is LearnHarness without its setup (AE2-1).
  • Primary persona Tayo; secondary Maya; anti-personas Sam and Dev (see Personas tab).
  • Nine screens, one nudge: as little design as possible.
Syllabus: UCD loop, now between Define and Ideate/Prototype (Wk 1); Double Diamond, start of Develop (Wk 5); Rams' tenth principle (Wk 5).
Open question for the team: which screens become alternatives in Jishnu's lo-fi (T17)?

First taste of value

  • Value before onboarding, per the 10 Nov decision.
  • LearnHarness took six steps to reach a first question (AE2-1); onboarding felt four times its length (AE2-P14, AE2-P15).
  • Opens with a question, not a blank page (AFF-A).
Syllabus: learnability (Wk 1); answer chips use recognition over recall (Wk 4); the question itself is Wk 4 mental models.
To test: does one question convince, or does it feel like a trick?

Three questions, then stop

  • Timetable is acceptable data, location isn't (Q-10).
  • Strictness is the student's choice (LIT-10, LIT-11) and can be changed (INT-05-h).
  • Nothing Dev would want here: no model choice, no upload.
Syllabus: chunking, three items is well inside short-term memory (Wk 4); all taps, recognition over recall (Wk 4); efficiency (Wk 1).

Weekly blocks you can move

  • Student-chosen times form an implementation intention (LIT-3, T20).
  • Weekly cadence and adjustable blocks (INT-05-h).
  • Claim routine, not a cure: habits take weeks (LIT-12).
  • A moved block stays outlined and labelled, so the change can't slip past.
Syllabus: change blindness (Wk 4); functional requirement "suggest and move blocks" (Wk 1); task-artifact cycle, watch how students repurpose blocks once adopted (Wk 1).
To test: do students accept suggested blocks, or want to place them from scratch?

One nudge, well timed

  • Welcome in a free gap after a lecture (Q-8, INT-05-g).
  • More than one or two a day and notifications get turned off (Q-9).
  • The copy names the tiniest first step (INT-04-e). It matches T20 nudge N-1.
Syllabus: context awareness, using the timetable (Wk 1); a notification sits in peripheral vision, so it says one thing in one line (Wk 4); non-functional requirement: max 1 a day (Wk 1).

Where you are, tiny first step, opt-in focus

  • Environment check (new). Place commits students: the library (INT-03-d, INT-05-b). Friends and social spaces derail sessions (INT-06-c, AFF-B). A phone in reach costs attention even when idle (LIT-6). So the block asks where you are and suggests a quieter spot or moving the phone away.
  • Self-reported, never sensed: students won't share location (Q-10).
  • "The very, very first, tiniest step" (INT-04-e, INT-01-e).
  • The phone is the main pull in 5 of 6 interviews (INT-03-b, INT-04-b, INT-06-c; LIT-9). But the lock is resisted (INT-04-g, INT-06-h), so it's opt-in, three levels, with an exit (X-8).
Syllabus: environmental requirements (Wk 1); context awareness (Wk 1); O'Kane's environment theme, too noisy, nowhere to sit (Wk 3); IoT-style sensing deliberately not used (Wk 2); lateral thinking, component alteration: swap "lock the phone" for "change the place" (Wk 5); the real lock is tested Wizard of Oz style (Wk 3).
Weakest evidence on the screen. Lekan: does the environment prompt feel helpful or like nagging? Is "Quiet" enough?

Learning engine (Jiven's area)

  • The student produces the answer (AFF-D, Q-6). Sam's "give me the answer" is refused.
  • Confidence slider and reasoning box kept from LearnHarness (AE2-5, AE2-8).
  • "Show me first" for students who want an example (Q-7); preference varies by subject (INT-04-h).
  • Source slide linked, because trust in AI is split (INT-01-g, INT-02-i).
  • Progress as numbered dots with ticks, and "End" is always visible.
Syllabus: never colour alone, ticks and numbers carry the state (Wk 4); chunks of 5 (Wk 4); safety, always an exit (Wk 1); effectiveness (Wk 1).

Breaks that wait for you

  • A fixed Pomodoro timer broke one participant's flow (INT-03-c).
  • Breaks turn into 20 minutes of scrolling (INT-03-b), so the break has a chosen length and one buzz at the end.
Syllabus: utility vs efficiency trade-off (Wk 1); design as a wicked problem, rest vs momentum has no single right answer (Wk 4).
Open: should "Keep going" be offered at all, or does it undercut rest?

Done, and forgiving

  • Apps come back before the summary: the session's goal is met, so anything left undone after it gets forgotten.
  • Show what's next so finishing isn't a cue to stop for days (INT-02-c).
  • Punishing streaks cause abandonment (INT-01-i): no streak, no backlog.
  • The missed question resurfaces (AFF-E), matching T20 nudge N-2.
Syllabus: closure and post-completion error, the cash machine returns your card first (Wk 4); memorability after a gap (Wk 1).

Settings: where Dev's wishes go

  • Nudge cap visible and editable (Q-9).
  • A plain list of the data used; location never (Q-10).
  • Advanced options exist but stay out of the start flow (anti-persona Dev).
Syllabus: anti-persona (Wk 3); "Locked" should match the mental model students already have from Screen Time and Opal, so learning transfers (Wk 4).

Task analysis: starting to study

Now: lecture ends → walk to hub → phone out → "I'll start later" → evening → maybe start, phone beside the laptop → break → scroll.
With LockedIN: lecture ends → one nudge → open → "where are you?" → move or settle → first question → set → break on your terms → apps back → next block already booked.

Wizard of Oz

A real lock needs native Screen Time access. In lo-fi testing a teammate swaps paper screens to fake "Quiet" and "Locked", so we learn whether students accept them before anyone builds them.

Six hats on the environment prompt

HatThought
White (facts)3 interviewees name place as a commitment or a derailer; LIT-6 shows a phone in reach costs attention
Red (feelings)Could feel like being told off for studying in bed
Black (risks)Nags; adds a tap before the first question; untestable without real contexts
Yellow (benefits)Cheap to build, no tracking, targets the cause rather than the symptom
Green (alternatives)Ask once per week instead of each block; remember the usual spot
Blue (process)Test as one variable in T18: block start with and without the prompt

Gould and Lewis

Early focus on users: personas and scenarios come before screens. Empirical measurement: the measures in the usability goals table. Iterative design: T18's results revise this sketch or replace it.
