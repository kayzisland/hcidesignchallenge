# Decision brief: what do we build the hi-fi on?

## The question
On 28 Oct the group decided to build the hi-fi on LearnHarness. That decision was made before any data existed. The questionnaire (n = 22) and two autoethnography sessions now exist and they cut both ways, so the decision is reopened once and settled here. On 8 Nov the design log moved the base from settled to **provisional** pending this meeting.

## What the evidence says, before the options
- **The problem we are designing for is starting, not understanding.** Q-1, Q-2, Q-11 and AFF-A all point the same way, and C-1 in the findings register is the best-evidenced claim the project has. Q-3 says understanding is the least frequent problem, picked by 1 of 22.
- **LearnHarness is strong at exactly the thing our users need least.** `../research/existing-solutions.md` scores it ✓ on recall and ◐ on mode, and ✗ on starting, focus, proactivity and course context. Those four are our design's whole claim.
- **One session with it confirmed that.** AE2-10: it teaches well and does nothing about starting. AE2-1: six setup steps before the first question. AE2-11: nothing in the session needed a separate application.
- **Two things in it are genuinely worth keeping.** AE2-5 and AE2-8: a confidence rating and a reasoning field beside one question, used unprompted and praised without being asked. This is the only part of either prototype walked on 8 Nov that produced enthusiasm (the other is AE2-P10, the live theme preview).
- **The counter-evidence to AE2-11 is Q-6.** General assistants are the most used tool in our sample (15 of 22) and among the least trusted (rated best by 2). If a general assistant were sufficient, students' experience of general assistants should be better than it is. That is X-3 in the register and it is unresolved.

## Option A: keep LearnHarness as the teaching engine, build our start-and-nudge layer around it

**In practice.** Jiven forks the repo and adds the layer our research actually supports: timetable sensing, a nudge scheduler inside the 1-2 a day budget, a session opener that states its goal, and the resumable short session. LearnHarness handles teaching and practice underneath.

**The case against.**
Our marks come from the start layer and this option spends the build on integration. LearnHarness is a Next.js application with a large dependency tree, its own agent loop, an MCP bridge and a provider abstraction; it needed Node 22, a CLI update and a provider opt-in before it would run at all (AE2-1). Nobody in the group has yet established what is reusable, because T11 has not landed. The engine's strength addresses Q-3, our smallest need. AE2-9 says its opening measure phase reads as a test, which is the opposite of what Q-1 and AFF-A require, so the first thing a user meets would be the thing we have evidence against. Under rubric 6 we would be shipping a large body of behaviour we did not design and cannot fully explain.

**The case for.**
The teaching is real, so a usability test (T23) tests something that works. It preserves the 28 Oct decision and whatever Jiven has already learned. AE2-5 and AE2-8 come free. It is the most impressive demo if the integration lands.

**Build effort before Tue 24 Nov.** Unknown, which is the point. T11 exists to answer it. Realistically two weeks of one person's time with a hard dependency on someone else's architecture.

**Rubric.** Helps 7 (domain understanding, a real engine). **Risks 6** (unexplained features we did not design) and **risks 8** (we cannot trace LearnHarness's own behaviour to our research).

## Option B: drop it and build the session flow straight on an LLM API with our own prompts

**In practice.** A small application of our own. Our prompts drive the question flow, our code owns the session, the nudges and the timetable sensing.

**The case against.**
This builds the teaching layer from scratch, which is the layer Q-3 says matters least, and it invites AE2-11's objection in its purest form: a thin wrapper over a general assistant is exactly what the participant said he could do without. It spends the most build time on the least-evidenced part of the design. Prompt-driven teaching is also the hardest thing to make reliable in a demo, and an unreliable demo in front of a marker is worse than a scripted one.

**The case for.**
Full control, so every element traces to a finding (rubric 8). No dependency on another codebase. Lighter than A.

**Build effort before Tue 24 Nov.** Moderate, perhaps ten days, with real risk that the teaching quality is poor and absorbs the time.

**Rubric.** Helps 8 (full traceability). **Risks 6** (we would be building a feature set our data ranks lowest) and risks 3 if the demo is flaky.

## Option C: a hi-fi that concentrates on the start, nudge and session layer, with a scripted teaching loop for demo purposes

**In practice.** The hi-fi does for real everything our research supports: senses the timetable, fires a nudge in a free gap inside the budget, opens straight into a session with its goal stated, runs a short resumable session with a confidence rating and a reasoning field, logs misses and resurfaces them. The teaching content inside the session is scripted from the Game Theory and HCI material we already have, with the mode switch (worked example or questioned through) genuinely working. The script is declared in the deck and on the poster.

**The case against.**
The teaching is not real, so T23 cannot test whether the teaching works, only whether the flow does. A marker could read a scripted loop as avoiding the hard part, and if we fail to declare it clearly it looks like concealment, which would damage criterion 8 far more than the script itself. It also throws away the 28 Oct decision and any work already done on the LearnHarness base.

**The case for.**
It puts the entire build on the part of the design every piece of our evidence supports (C-1, Q-1, Q-2, Q-8, Q-9, Q-10, AFF-A, AFF-F), and it removes the dependency risk entirely. Scripted hi-fi prototypes are a standard UCD technique, not a shortcut, as long as the scripting is stated: what is being evaluated is the interaction, and Week 9's usability testing material assumes exactly this kind of prototype. Every element would trace to a finding ID, which is what criterion 8 is marked on. AE2-5 and AE2-8 can be borrowed as an interaction pattern without taking the engine. It is also the only option that is certainly finishable by 24 Nov, which protects T23, T24 and the deck.

**Build effort before Tue 24 Nov.** Lowest and most predictable. Roughly a week for the flow, a few days for content.

**Rubric.** Helps **6** (nothing in it is unexplained), **8** (every element cites a finding), 3 and 4 (a working flow to test). **Risks 7** if the scripting makes the domain look thin, and risks 8 **only if the script is not declared**.

## Option D: A and C combined

**In practice.** The build effort goes on the start layer, because that is where the evidence is strongest. That layer is four things:

1. **Calendar sync.** The app reads the timetable, which Q-10 permits (21 of 22) and which nothing else in the table does for a student's actual course.
2. **Student-chosen weekly study blocks.** The student picks the times. The app does not.
3. **A Pomodoro-style phone lock during a block,** with the blocked apps opening during a 5-minute break every 25 minutes. **Simulated in the prototype**, because a real lock needs native Screen Time access we are not going to build in two weeks.
4. **The post-lecture nudge** already specified in `../define/behaviour-change.md` as N-1.

Inside a block, the student learns with LearnHarness, made usable for a student who is not a developer:

- **No setup at all.** Course material pre-loaded, no CLI, no model choice, no provider opt-in. AE2-1 is six setup steps before the first question, and that is the single thing that must not survive into our build.
- **Teach in the first minute,** and fold the measuring into the teaching. AE2-9: sustained probing before any explanation read as an exam.
- **Show progress during waits, and introduce a notation before using it.** AFF-H is the whole cluster: AE2-2, AE2-4, AE2-7.
- **Keep the confidence rating and the reasoning box.** AE2-5 and AE2-8 are the only part of either walked tool that produced unprompted enthusiasm.

**Fallback, and it is the point of D.** If Jiven's T11 spike shows by **Fri 13 Nov** that integrating LearnHarness costs too much, the session runs a scripted loop that copies LearnHarness's flow instead. The demo never depends on the integration landing. D is A with C as a committed fallback, decided on a date, not a hope.

**How D answers AE2-11.** The objection was "I don't see it doing anything that Claude couldn't do". Say the answer plainly, in the deck and at the viva: **a chat assistant cannot read your timetable, hold your study blocks, lock your phone, or arrive unprompted after a lecture.** Those four are the product. LearnHarness is the engine behind them, and the engine was never the differentiator. That is also what `../research/existing-solutions.md` now shows from the other direction: blockers do the locking and never teach, and tutors teach and never start you.

**The case against.**
It is two builds in two weeks. The start layer and a LearnHarness integration are each a fortnight's work on their own, and the fallback means potentially building the session twice. The lock is simulated, so the hi-fi cannot demonstrate the one mechanism the concept rests on, and T23 will test a mock of the central feature. Worst of all, **phone distraction is not confirmed in our own data.** X-6 in the findings register is unresolved: LIT-9 is a meta-analysis over 8,679 students linking phone use to procrastination, and not one of our 8 Q12 answers mentioned the phone. D commits the build to a mechanism justified by other people's students until our interviews say otherwise.

**The case for.**
It is the only option that puts the build where C-1 points and still keeps the two things the LearnHarness session actually proved were good (AE2-5, AE2-8). It answers AE2-11 with a product fact, which no amount of arguing can do. The fallback caps the downside at a known date, which is what A lacked: A's problem was never that it was wrong, it was that its cost was unknown. D makes the cost knowable by 13 Nov and survivable either way.

**Build effort before Tue 24 Nov.** The start layer is about a week. The LearnHarness integration is unknown until T11, which is exactly why the 13 Nov checkpoint exists. The scripted fallback is a few days on top of the start layer.

**Rubric.** Helps **6** (every feature traces to a finding and the simulated lock is declared), **8** (the AE2-11 answer is stated plainly and the simulation is admitted), **7** (a real engine, if the integration lands), 3 and 4. **Risks 9** (management: two builds is the most ambitious plan on the table) and **risks 3** if the simulated lock makes T23's results thin.

## Concept proposal: study blocks + phone lock (KAYZ, 9 Nov)

**Proposed by KAYZ for group review. Not a decision.** Option D builds on this, and it could equally be adopted on its own as one of Jishnu's lo-fi alternatives for T17.

**The concept.** The student picks two fixed study times a week. The app syncs their timetable, holds those blocks, and during a block locks the distracting apps on their phone, opening them for a 5-minute break every 25 minutes. Inside the block they get a short session on what they were actually taught that week.

**Why the evidence points here.**
- **Starting is the problem** (Q-1, Q-2, and convergence C-1 across four methods). A block that exists before the moment arrives removes the decision to start.
- **A pre-committed block is an implementation intention** (LIT-3, and the argument is already made in `../define/behaviour-change.md` section 2.3). The student forms the if-then once a week, and the system executes it. Q-11's failures are goal intentions with no if-then attached.
- **The timetable is permitted and the course context is ours alone** (Q-10: timetable 21 of 22, location 3 of 22). No blocker in `../research/existing-solutions.md` knows what you are studying.
- **Students already work in blocks** (AE-11: set deep-work blocks plus spontaneous urgent bursts).
- **Phones are a credible driver of failed starts** (LIT-6 to LIT-9). LIT-6 found the mere presence of a phone costs cognitive capacity even when it is silent and face down, and LIT-7 found students on task about 65% of a 15-minute session with focus slipping after about two minutes.

**Cautions, all of which constrain the design.**
- **Claim routine modestly.** LIT-12 puts habit formation at a median of 66 days, range 18 to 254. One term is not enough for most students. The claim is a lower starting cost, never that the starting problem disappears.
- **The student sets the strictness and keeps an override.** LIT-10 found 280 students still using self-control tools at 1 to 3 months when they chose their own goals and tools, and picked very different combinations. LIT-11 found goal reminders were often called annoying and feed removal caused fear of missing out, and recommends flexible blocking matched to each person's definition of distraction. A lock with no way out is the wrong design, and it is also exactly what Opal's Hard Mode does.
- **Fixed blocks clash with two of our own findings.** AE2-P8 is a participant rejecting fixed session lengths and taking "choose each time" as the only honest answer. AE-11 says study happens in bursts as well as blocks. This is **X-7** in `../research/findings-register.md`. The concept survives it only if blocks are movable, skippable and set by the student.
- **Our own data has never mentioned phones** (X-6). The interview guide now asks directly, in section 5b. If the interviews come back without phones in them, this concept loses its main justification and should be dropped, not defended.

## KAYZ's proposed position

**Proposed by KAYZ for group review. Not a decision.**

**Take option D.**

The reason is the division of marks. Eight of the ten criteria reward the research-to-design chain and the honesty of it, and one rewards the demo. Our chain is strongest, by a distance, on starting and nudging: C-1 rests on four methods, and every one of Q-1, Q-2, Q-11, AFF-A, AE2-P14, AE2-P15, AE2-1 and AE2-10 says the same thing. Our chain is weakest on teaching, where Q-3 says the need is smallest and the only direct evidence we have about an AI tutor is one session that said it does not solve our problem. C's instinct was right: spend the build where the evidence is. A and B both spend it where the evidence is not.

**D keeps that and adds back the part of LearnHarness the evidence does support.** AE2-5 and AE2-8 are real: a confidence rating and a reasoning field beside one question were the only things in either walked tool that drew unprompted praise. C threw those away along with the engine. D keeps the start layer as the build's centre and keeps the engine behind it, with the four usability fixes our own sessions demand (AE2-1, AE2-9, AFF-H).

D also beats C on the hardest question we will be asked. AE2-11 is "this could just be Claude", and C's answer is a scripted loop, which invites it again. D's answer is a product fact: a chat assistant cannot read your timetable, hold your blocks, lock your phone or arrive after a lecture.

**What made A unusable was never that it was wrong. It was that its cost was unknown.** D fixes that with a date. If T11 shows by Fri 13 Nov that the integration is too expensive, the session falls back to C's scripted loop and nothing else changes. That is the whole argument for D over both: it takes A's upside with C's floor.

**What would change my mind:** if the interviews this week come back with no mention of phones or study blocks, the concept loses its justification (X-6) and D collapses back to C with a nudge layer. That is a factual question the interviews answer, and they run before the hi-fi build starts at T21.

**Three things to carry over whichever option wins,** because they are evidenced and cheap:
1. The confidence rating and reasoning field beside a single question (AE2-5, AE2-8).
2. A session that opens with its goal stated and the first question already on screen, so there is no blank page (AE2-P1, AE2-1, Q-11).
3. A visible live effect for any preference the user sets (AE2-P10).

**And one to avoid whichever option wins:** do not open with a measure phase. AE2-9 says sustained probing before any teaching reads as an exam, and AFF-A says the first job is getting the student in.
