## Findings
- **INT-01-a:** Studies week by week to keep pace. Being on time takes away the reason to work ahead, so effort only rises once he falls behind. ("if on time doesn't see point in getting ahead, no immediate benefit")
- **INT-01-b:** He manages attention through his surroundings, not apps. He keeps his laptop and phone away in lectures, works in the library because the walk there commits him, and leaves phone notifications uncleared as a standing reminder. He doesn't use trackers. ("Leave notifications on phone - don't click/get rid of, constant reminder")
- **INT-01-c:** Whether he follows up depends on how much he understood. If he follows less than about 80% of a lecture, he struggles to follow up at all. ("if he struggles to understand less than a certain threshold of a lecture i.e. 80% struggles to follow up")
- **INT-01-d:** He wants to go straight to what he didn't understand. Each lecture should be broken into small sections that still cover all of it, so he can pick the section he struggled with. Doing 10 cards on weak topics beats doing 100 to find them. ("would rather do 10 on topics they struggle with")
- **INT-01-e:** Lecture material comes with no order. He can't tell what to do first or how topics relate, which turns studying into an "insurmountable task". He wants a roadmap with an order. ("No standardised roadmap to actually learning the info. Ordering preferred.")
- **INT-01-f:** As well as a roadmap, he would perfer to reach any piece of information directly, through clearly defined, organised sections and an intuitive search method. A tool that makes him go the long way round is no use to him. ("Product becomes redundant if you have to get to it in a roundabout way.")
- **INT-01-g:** He needs to trace everything back to the original source. He doesn't trust LLMs as a source of truth, so every summary should link to the exact slide or moment in the lecture video for checking. ("doesn't trust them as a base source of truth")
- **INT-01-h:** He finds transcripts useful, but Panopto's interface and inaccurate transcript make 50 minutes of lecture impossible to search. ("can't find through/sift through 50 mins of a lecturer transcript")
- **INT-01-i (abandonment):** He almost gave up on his current flashcard app. Any card left for 17 days was marked as not completed, so more than 2,000 cards lapsed and keeping up would have meant over 100 cards a day. Starting again felt demoralising. Progress has to be recoverable after a long break, "like the gym". ("Progress system can't be vindictive/ overly penalising.")

## Full notes by topic

### How he studies and tracks progress
- He compared how prepared he feels with where he should be relative to the course.
- He studies methodically, week by week. If he falls behind, he catches up. If he is on time, he sees no point in getting ahead, because there is no immediate benefit.
- He tracks progress by week, page and lecture.
- He doesn't use trackers or notifications as study tools.
- He leaves phone notifications on and doesn't clear them, so they act as a constant reminder.

### Focus, distraction and procrastination
- He doesn't want to be pulled away from the task he is doing. He struggles to follow through straight away and is prone to procrastinating.
- He can usually focus in lectures. If he follows less than a certain amount of a lecture (he suggested about 80%), he struggles to follow up afterwards.
- He focuses better in lectures without a laptop or phone, because there is less to distract him.
- He prefers working in the library. Having already put in the walk there, and dreading the walk home, keeps him there: "in for a penny in for a pound".

### Lecture materials and Panopto
- He finds the transcript useful but doesn't like the Panopto interface.
- With an inaccurate transcript, it's arduous to search or sift through 50+ minutes of a lecture.

### The core problem: no order and no roadmap
- The information isn't sequential. He doesn't know what order to go through it in, what order to do tasks in, or which topics relate to each other.
- His motivation competes with the feeling that the task is insurmountable.
- There is no standard roadmap for actually learning the material, and he would like one with an order.

### Finding information
- He compared this to an open-world game versus a story-based game. He should be able to find any piece of information, because the material isn't standardised.
- If he has to get to the product the long way round, it becomes redundant.
- He suggested something similar to a file system with a search function, so he can find the information he needs easily.

### Trust and checking sources
- He wants links to a specific part of a slide or a Panopto video, so he can check anything against the original. This removes any bias if he thinks a summary is wrong.
- He doesn't trust LLMs as a single source of truth.

### Learning formats and past tools
- He thinks MCQs and flashcards are a good idea. Appreciates visual cues.
- He used Seneca and Memrise in sixth form and liked their features for active recall due to "simple question to answer" mapping.
- Format matters less to him than breaking the information down into manageable portions he can choose between.

### His interface ideas (he raised these himself)
- He would click through lots of small cards, each holding a short section of text. Clicking one he doesn't understand would bring up an explanation.
- The app would automatically break each lecture into sets of flashcards, one set per section, so he can target the part he didn't understand.
- The sets should cover everything in the lecture rather than selecting specific parts.
- On selecting versus ranking: the app should make it easier for him to find and select the chunk he struggled with rather than have the system rank which parts were the hardest. 
- The app could standardise slide content by using font size to work out which sections matter most. This idea has limitations but semantic recognition is a good point to consider with respect to lecture slides.

### Almost abandoning his flashcard app
- In his flashcard app, a card not reviewed for 17 days was marked as not completed.
- At one point he had over 2,000 cards, and all of them ended up marked as not completed.
- Avoiding that would have meant reviewing over 100 cards a day. It didn't help him get organised, and starting again was very demoralising.
- Penalties need to be recoverable. If he doesn't use the app for two months, it should be easy to get back to where he was, like returning to the gym.
- The progress system mustn't be vindictive or overly punishing to users.

## Surprises (things that contradict the questionnaire or AE-01)
- **He uses the phone as a reminder, not a distraction.** The study-block concept, which locks the phone, would hide the notifications he relies on. This complicates X-6.
- **He prefers choosing for himself over the app choosing.** He would rather pick the chunk he struggled with than have the app rank topics or pull in explanations from online sources. This challenges the proactive angle and the AI-explanation angle.
- **He wants both a fixed order and free access.** He asked for a roadmap with an order, and also to be able to reach anything directly. The design needs to offer both, not choose between them.
- **Being on time takes away his motivation.** A post-lecture quiz offered while he is up to date may not land with him.
- **He distrusts LLMs, and LearnHarness is an AI tutor.** If the prototype keeps that base, every AI explanation needs a link back to the source.
