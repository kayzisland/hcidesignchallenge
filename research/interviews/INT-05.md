## Findings
- **INT-05-a:** They know how to study and lack the desire to start. Asked whether an app should teach studying or tell them when to study, they chose the second: they have "no desire to do it", not a skills gap.
- **INT-05-b:** Their last session: a couple of spare hours after placement, so they went to the library. About an hour passed between deciding and starting, mostly travel. Being at the library mattered "even if they did no work".
- **INT-05-c:** No clear finish point. They stopped when they ran out of time.
- **INT-05-d:** Their phone distracted them several times. They came back to work after getting bored of it or turning it off.
- **INT-05-e:** They use Anki on an iPad, with cards made by a student in the year above. They write the answer, then mark wrong ones to see again.
- **INT-05-f:** Shown versus asked depends on the subject. With a set goal they want to be told first, then replicate it. Without one they want to try first, then improve. They check understanding by answering more questions and by confidence, and they say there's no objective measure.
- **INT-05-g:** A 10-minute post-lecture quiz in a free hour: they'd do it and think it would be "really, really great", if limited to one notification. Repeated reminders would "get on my nerves".
- **INT-05-h:** Declaring free study times: they'd use it only if the times could be adjusted, because their schedule fluctuates a lot. They plan availability in weeks and prefer a weekly cadence. Most useful in holidays and long free periods.

## Surprises
- **The clearest statement of our core problem (INT-05-a):** a student who can study, and doesn't start.
- **Location is a commitment device (INT-05-b).** Going somewhere to study works even when no studying happens. INT-01, INT-03 and INT-06 say the same about the library.
