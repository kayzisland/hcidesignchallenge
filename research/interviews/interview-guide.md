# Interview guide: 15-minute study interviews

Status: v1, 8 Nov 2026. Interviewer: KAYZ. Participants: questionnaire volunteers (Q16). Format: semi-structured, online, recorded and transcribed with Notion AI **with consent**. Each interview gets an ID (`INT-01`...) and its findings get IDs (`INT-01-a`...).

## Why interviews, and why semi-structured
The questionnaire told us *what* (getting started is the top problem, Q-1). It can't tell us *why*. Interviews give the why, in the participant's own words (Wk 2). Semi-structured keeps the questions comparable across participants while leaving room to follow what they raise. We dropped observation (see `../../design-log.md`, 8 Nov), so these interviews also carry the "last real session" question, which gets as close to behaviour as recall allows.

## Rules for the interviewer (Wk 2)
- Talk 5-15% of the time. Silence is a question.
- Probe without prompting: "Tell me more about that", "What happened next?", "Why do you think that was?" Never "Was it because you were tired?"
- Ask about **the last real time**, not "usually".
- Don't pitch the app. Don't describe our design until the last section.

## Before recording (read out)
> Thanks for doing this. It's 15 minutes about how you study. There are no right answers, and you can skip anything or stop at any time. I'd like to record and transcribe this with Notion AI so I don't have to take notes. The recording stays within our HCI group, your name won't appear in anything we submit, and quotes will be anonymised. Is it OK if I record?

Start recording only after a clear yes. Restate the consent on the recording.

## Questions (about 15 minutes)

**1. Warm-up (1 min)**
- What are you studying, and what year are you in?

**2. The last study session (5 min). Probes Q-1, Q-2, Q-11**
- Think about the last time you sat down to study. Walk me through it, from the moment you decided to study.
- How long was it between deciding to study and actually starting? What happened in that gap?
- What were you trying to get done in that session? How did you know when you were done?
- Did anything pull you off track? What did you do about it?

**3. When it goes wrong (3 min). Probes Q-1, Q-3, Q-11**
- Tell me about a session that went badly. What happened?
- (If they mention not starting) What would have had to be different for you to start?
- (If they mention not understanding) What did you do when you got stuck?

**4. How you learn (3 min). Probes Q-6, Q-7**
- What do you actually do when you study? Show me, if you can, the last thing you used.
- When something is hard, would you rather be shown how, or be asked questions until you get there? Why? Has that ever changed depending on the subject?
- How do you check you've really understood something?

**5. Reaction to a proactive app (2 min). Probes Q-8, Q-9, Q-10**
- Imagine your phone notices you have a free hour after a lecture and offers a 10-minute quiz on that lecture. What would you do?
- What would make that annoying? What would make it useful?

**6. Close (1 min)**
- Is there anything about how you study that I didn't ask but should have?
- Thank them. Stop the recording.

## After each interview (10 minutes, same day)
1. Save the Notion AI transcript and summary on the interview's Notion page.
2. Pull 3-6 findings, each with an ID and a short anonymised quote: `INT-01-a: [finding] ("quote")`.
3. Add `INT-01.md` to this folder (`research/interviews/`): participant profile (year, subject, ND yes/no/not sure, no name or email), findings and quotes.
4. Every finding goes on the affinity diagram (T13).
