## Findings
- **INT-04-a:** Their last session was exam crunch time. The gap between deciding to study and starting was short because of the deadline, and they studied all day on practice papers. Urgency removed the starting problem.
- **INT-04-b:** Their phone is "always right next to me" and "almost always" pulls them off track. Phone notifications and in-person interruptions are the main distractions, followed by boredom and tiredness.
- **INT-04-c:** A bad session is constant distraction. They get drawn into non-study tasks such as editing videos, hours pass, and then they need to eat or sleep.
- **INT-04-d:** Removing the phone would help, but they have a general problem with distraction and would "probably find another way".
- **INT-04-e:** Breaking a task down is their biggest struggle. They suggested an AI could split a known objective into actionable steps, "even just the very, very first, tiniest step".
- **INT-04-f:** A 10-minute quiz in a free hour after a lecture: positive, but annoying "if I don't want to do it". Timing it to when they're receptive, or letting them schedule it, would help.
- **INT-04-g:** A phone lock in fixed study times: they'd keep it on for urgent tasks and might switch it off for non-urgent ones.
- **INT-04-h:** Shown versus asked depends on the subject. In maths they want to be shown, after asking many questions. In philosophy being shown doesn't work; they ask questions and re-read. They check understanding by cross-referencing and with practice papers.

## Surprises
- **Urgency solves starting (INT-04-a).** The starting problem is worst when nothing is due. That points the app at non-urgent periods, which is when INT-04-g says the lock gets switched off.
- **A new feature request: break the task into the tiniest first step (INT-04-e).** It matches what the existing solutions review found in Goblin Tools.
