## Findings
- **INT-03-a:** Starting is the hardest part. They describe a "mental block" to starting, filled with social media (YouTube, Instagram), games and chores. Once they're in flow, it isn't as bad. ("mental block")
- **INT-03-b:** Social media is the main thing that pulls them off track. Breaks taken about every 50 minutes often turn into around 20 minutes of scrolling. Putting the phone away and walking or getting a snack on breaks helps, but they "sometimes still just do it".
- **INT-03-c:** They tried Pomodoro with a timer and dropped it, because the timer interrupted their flow, for example in the middle of a question set.
- **INT-03-d:** They moved from studying at home to the library. Home has family interruptions and is too comfortable, which led to procrastination. They plan the library day the night before, roughly weekdays 9 to 5, and are "not too strict" about it.
- **INT-03-e:** A bad session: friends were on campus, they ate together instead of going to the library, and the day was gone.
- **INT-03-f:** They use AI twice per session: a summary first, then the full reading, and AI-written multiple-choice questions at the end to test themselves. If they do badly, they revisit what they didn't understand, then move on.
- **INT-03-g:** Shown versus asked depends on interest. With interesting subjects they work it out themselves over several attempts. With less interesting ones they would rather be told.

## Surprises
- **Fixed Pomodoro breaks broke their flow (INT-03-c).** That cuts directly against the study-block concept's fixed 25-minute cycle. Breaks need to fit the work, or be skippable.
- **They already run the "measure at the end" loop themselves with AI MCQs (INT-03-f),** and they trust AI enough to use it for summaries. That contrasts with INT-01 and INT-02.
