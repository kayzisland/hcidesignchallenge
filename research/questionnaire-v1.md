# Questionnaire v1: choosing our problem

Status: draft for group review. Target: Birmingham students. Anonymous. About 5 minutes, 16 questions.

## What this questionnaire has to answer
1. Which of our five brainstormed problems (socialising, fitness, workload, money, safety) affects students most often?
2. Which one do students most want help with?
3. For safety, our current lead idea: how common are bike theft and burglary, and what do students already do to prevent them?
4. Will students accept a proactive system: alerts they didn't ask for, data access, and how many notifications a day?

Our open question goes last, so the closed ones don't get answered in a rush.

## Why each question is here (for the group discussion and the deck)

| Q | Answers research question | Why this form |
|---|---|---|
| 1-3 | Context | Lets us split results by year, housing and home/international. Q3 tests the socialising idea, which the doc ties to international students. Q2 tests whether safety is really a Selly Oak problem. |
| 4 | 1 | A recent, countable time frame (past month) gets a behaviour, not an opinion. Labelled frequency scale. "If at all" keeps the stem neutral. Home and travel safety are split so neither row is double-barrelled. |
| 5 | 2 | Forced single choice shows priority. Frequency alone doesn't. "None of these" stops false answers. |
| 6 | 2 | Gets the "why" behind the numbers, and seeds scenarios and personas. Optional, and last in its section, so it doesn't cause drop-outs. |
| 7 | 3 | Fact about events, not feelings. Tick boxes keep it fast. "Prefer not to say" because it's sensitive. Gives us the bike-theft figure that police data can't isolate. |
| 8-9 | 3 | Filter question, so non-cyclists aren't forced to answer. Overnight storage shows where theft risk sits. |
| 10 | 3, 4 | Current prevention behaviour, and the moment a proactive system could step in (leaving for the holidays). The last option covers students it doesn't apply to. |
| 11 | 3 | Perceived safety, which we compare with Q7's actual incidents. A balanced, labelled 5-point scale. |
| 12 | 4 | The brief requires proactive behaviour, so we test whether students welcome it. Examples span three ideas, so it doesn't favour one. |
| 13 | 4 | Which data sources are acceptable, which sets what's feasible. "None" option included. |
| 14 | 4 | Interruption tolerance, which sets the notification budget for the design. |
| 15 | Context | Platform choice for the prototype. |
| 16 | Next stage | Recruits for interviews (Empathise stage two) without breaking anonymity. |

## Pilot before sending
- Send to 5-8 students outside the group first.
- Ask each: which question did you have to reread, and what did you think it meant?
- Time it. If it runs over 6 minutes, cut questions.
- Log every change as v1 → v2 in this folder. That log is evidence for rubric criteria 2-4.

## Analysis plan
- Q4 x Q5: rank the problems by frequency and by priority. The idea we build should lead on both, or we explain why not.
- Split Q4 and Q7 by Q2 (housing area) and Q3 (home/international).
- Q11 against Q7: does perceived safety match actual incidents?
- Q12-Q14: limits on how proactive the system can be.
- Q6: code the open answers into themes, then use them for personas and scenarios.
